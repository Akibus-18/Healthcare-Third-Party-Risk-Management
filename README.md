# 📌 Executive Summary

This project simulates the implementation of a mature **Third-Party Risk Management (TPRM)** program for **Johns Hopkins Hospital (JHH)**.

The engagement was designed to identify, assess, remediate, and continuously monitor risks associated with vendors handling Protected Health Information (ePHI), supporting clinical operations, or providing critical healthcare technology services.

The project followed a structured six-week lifecycle aligned with industry-leading healthcare and cybersecurity frameworks including **HIPAA, HITECH, HITRUST CSF, NIST SP 800-53, and ISO/IEC 27001**.

## Key Outcomes

✅ Assessed 59 Active Vendors

✅ Identified 9 Vendors Requiring Immediate Action

✅ Closed 3 High-Risk Security Gaps

✅ Improved Compliance Posture from 67% to 92%

✅ Established Continuous Monitoring Processes

✅ Implemented Vendor Onboarding & Offboarding Standards

✅ Delivered Executive-Level Risk Reporting

---

# 🎯 Business Problem

Healthcare organizations rely on hundreds of third-party vendors to support:

* Electronic Health Records (EHR)
* Cloud Infrastructure
* Medical Devices
* Telehealth Services
* Identity & Access Management
* Revenue Cycle Management

A compromised vendor can expose organizations to:

* HIPAA Violations
* Patient Safety Risks
* Data Breaches
* Operational Disruptions
* Regulatory Penalties
* Financial Losses

The objective of this project was to build a scalable, repeatable, and compliance-driven TPRM framework capable of identifying and mitigating vendor-related cybersecurity and privacy risks before they impact patient care.
